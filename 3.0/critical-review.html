BAGWAN REFRESH · 20 NOVEMBER 2026

Critical review of Bagwan

A review of every migrated page, the main functional journeys and the experience of using Bagwan as a hospital intranet.

The technical migration is broadly working. The larger risks are content quality, dated references, incomplete procedures and a mix of workplace information and learning interactions. The 3.0 preview addresses navigation and presentation while retaining working records and the tour.

What was checked

Every existing HTML route was loaded locally at desktop and phone sizes, with content inventories and screenshots. Local navigation, downloads, section links and runtime assets were checked. A fresh axe scan covered 102 existing pages; the standalone panorama was excluded from that scan. Keyboard journeys passed 42 checks and clinical form journeys passed 51 checks. The first blue 3.0 review build passed 55 journey checks; layout and accessibility results are recorded in the verification section below.

This is a critical product and learning-design review, not clinical approval or a claim of full WCAG conformance. The browser checks used Chromium. Screen readers, Safari, Firefox, physical devices, downloaded-document accessibility and full video playback require further review.

Review before teaching

The archived waste answer key was ambiguous

The old activity put an unspecified expired medicine in cytotoxic waste. An expiry date does not establish that classification. The new draft uses an explicit cytotoxic example and a separate insufficient-information example. The SME must approve the categories and feedback.

Review before teaching

Clinical policy content is incomplete or unverified

Medication safety and handover had no supplied policy; booking an interpreter was an empty template. Drafts now exist. Complete code of conduct and privacy text was restored from supplied Word documents. Existing source policies still require clinical, legal and approval review.

High

Current and archived information were hard to distinguish

Old news could look like a current hospital alert. 3.0 labels news as archive material and labels newly authored policy content as draft. No current clinical approval or live hospital status has been invented.

High

Nine external URLs returned 404

All nine were also linked from WordPress. Verified current resources replace these links in 3.0 and in the local repair set. A changed web address does not validate the clinical or legal content of the linked policy.

Medium

Ten departmental charts overflowed on phones

The local charts now use responsive layouts. Their existing names, roles and reporting groups have been preserved. The blue 3.0 content views use responsive reference layouts.

Medium

Finding information required knowing the site structure

Policies, forms, documents and resources were spread across nested menus and duplicate indexes. 3.0 provides task-based navigation and search across pages, people, policies, documents and video.

Medium

Some interactions worked against the intranet context

Values were hidden behind flip controls and safety content used picture dragging. Values are now visible reference text. PPE and waste references are in the intranet; optional HTML practice is in the separate learning guide.

Medium

Long policies and embedded pages increased effort

Twelve original policy pages exceeded 1400 words. 3.0 uses readable line lengths and section navigation where headings are available. Nested department resource frames become dedicated resource links.

Medium

The draft and evidence lifecycle needed clearer boundaries

Forms save on the device rather than submitting to a hospital or LMS. The new interface explains this and adds a clear-local-draft action. The learning guide explains where reflection and evidence belong.

Items that still need a person to check

  • AustLII and Cole-Parmer returned 403 to automated requests. They are not confirmed dead links.
  • The archived video Example of a Poorly-Managed Meeting did not provide public oEmbed metadata. Its watch link is retained for manual checking. The other 23 videos returned metadata; that does not prove embedding permission or complete playback.
  • The current NAATI directory is linked from NAATI but its separate directory service blocked the automated check. A human should verify the directory task.
  • The counselling panorama and evacuation-plan image were unavailable at the source. No hospital geography or evacuation route has been invented.
  • The recovered nursing charts and liaison roster supply staff names. The Emergency nursing reporting line, liaison contacts, clinical procedures and other source discrepancies need SME and SLD review. Read the reporting-line questions.
  • Downloaded Word and PDF files retain their source content. Their internal links, annotations and accessibility need a separate document review.

Learning design recommendations

Keep the staff intranet authentic and put instructions, reflection, evidence requirements and facilitator prompts in the separate guide. Start a session with a role and a workplace problem, then let learners find and use the relevant information. Avoid adding a score or reveal interaction when the actual task is to locate a reference.

Before teaching use, Andrew and the SME should agree the learner level, scenario brief, relevant units, evidence criteria and debrief questions. Test whether a learner can find the right policy, identify a colleague, locate a record and explain a decision without interface coaching. The proposed practice activities support discussion; they do not establish competence.

Build and review plan

  1. Restore and stabilise. Recover gallery sources, repair confirmed links, fix mobile chart layouts and restore source policy text.
  2. Build the parallel staff intranet. Keep Bagwan blue, introduce a Material-inspired visual language, improve search and navigation, and preserve working forms and the hospital tour.
  3. Separate learning support. Replace the three H5P packages with optional native HTML activities and make values readable in the intranet.
  4. Review new content. SME and SLD review all ten drafts, their answer keys, cultural appropriateness and scenario alignment. Record decisions and corrections in the register.
  5. Validate with representative learners. Observe realistic tasks, check screen readers and target devices, review document accessibility and third-party resources, then decide on a wider release.

Visual and interaction direction

The visual thesis is a recognisable blue hospital workspace with clear Material-inspired surfaces, strong typography and restrained depth. The content hierarchy is staff tasks first, then hospital updates, services and departmental detail. Drawer motion, selected navigation states and press feedback support orientation; reduced-motion preferences are respected.
