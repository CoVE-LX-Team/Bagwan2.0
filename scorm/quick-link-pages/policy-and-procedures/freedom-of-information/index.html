Freedom of information

Policy Name – Freedom of Information
Policy Number
Version Number
 
Scope

This policy applies to all staff and contractors employed by Bagwan Health, Allied Health professionals, Credentialed Specialists, students, patients and visitors.

Policy

The Freedom of Information Act 1982 (Vic) gives members of the public the right to access their records held by Bagwan Health. The Act also allows members of the public to request their records to be annotated or to request an explanation or summary of their records.

This policy defines the standards and processes associated with the request to access records under the Freedom of Information Act 1982 (Vic).

Procedure 

Most Freedom of Information (FOI) requests involve members of the public seeking access to documents containing their personal information. Individuals can also request access to documents containing other information, such as information about government policies, programs and decision-making processes.Categories of Documents

Categories of Documents

In the course of running Bagwan Health and carrying out our functions, a range of documents are created. The following is a non-exhaustive list of the types of documents that are either publicly available or available for access under the Freedom of Information Act 1982 (Vic):

·         Medical records

·         Contracts

·         Policies and procedures

·         Briefings and reports

·         Human resource records

·         Official correspondence

·         Meeting records

·         Financial records

·         Clinical training, research and education materials

 

·         Media releases.

Bagwan Health documents can be organised under the following categories:

·         Clinical operations – treatment records

·         Research and clinical training operations

·         Procurement and supply chain

·         Facilities and utilities

·         Business and strategic planning

·         Human resources

·         Finance

·         Legal

 

·         Policy.

Types of Patient Information

Medical and health information is collected during inpatient, outpatient or emergency attendances. The information is used to assist patients in their ongoing care and treatment. Such information includes details of current and past illnesses or injuries, operations, pathology results and medications taken/prescribed. 

Medical and health information is stored in many ways including paper, computer systems, film, tapes and x-rays.

Record keeping

Bagwan Health complies with the statutory requirements for information retention according to the Public Records Office of Victoria. The table below outlines the retention period for medical and health records.

Type of records

Retention period

Inpatient records

Generally 15 years after last attendance, or until the age of 25, whichever is the longest

Outpatient and emergency department records

Minimum of 12 years and up to 20 years from the last date of attendance

Psychiatric inpatient and outpatient records

Minimum of 25 years or until the age of 43, whichever is the longest

Radiology

Minimum of 5 years after creation

Records of deceased patients

12 years from the date of death

 

At the end of the retention period, records are disposed of in a secure manner.


Applications to Access Records

In accordance with the Freedom of Information Act (1992), all applications to access records must:

·         Be in writing (email and facsimile are acceptable)

·         Include sufficient detail to identify the correct documents covered by the request (e.g. full name of patient, date of birth; date of attendance or services accessed)

·         Give a mailing address in Australia where notices under the Act can be sent

·         Be lodged at Bagwan Health with any application fee payable under the regulations

 

·         Be accompanied by copies of two forms of ID e.g. drivers’ licence, Medicare card etc. 

Applications to access records must be made either in writing to the FOI Officer or by completing an Application Form from the Health Information Services department.

Non-personal Applications

Members of the public can obtain another person’s records if they can provide written authorisation from the person permitting access to their information.

If the application is for somebody that is deceased, then the applicant must provide identification that indicates that they are the closest relative to that person. If the applicant is not the closest relative, the applicant must provide written authorisation from the closest living relative permitting them access to the information.

A person may also obtain another person’s records if they can provide evidence that they:

·         Have Power of Attorney (medical)

 

·         Are a Legal Guardian of that person.

Can records be released outside FOI?

Records may be released to other healthcare providers for the continuing provision of health care (e.g. Emergency Department) and may also be released in accordance with due legal process (e.g. as evidence in a legal action before a court).

Cost

There are no fees or charges of the application if a member of the public requests their own personal information. An application fee of $30 is required for non-personal applications, and this must be paid when the application is lodged. Additional charges such as photocopying and staff time may be incurred, as per the Freedom of Information Act 1992.

Bagwan Health may waive or reduce the application fee in some instances, e.g. financial hardship, Health Care Card and Pension Card holders.

Notice of Decision

Upon receipt of a valid application, Bagwan Health has a maximum of 45 calendar days to make a decision regarding access. At that time, the requester may also receive an invoice for any applicable charges. The charges must be paid in full before any documents are released. There may be circumstances where Bagwan Health will need to consult with the requester to extend the 30 day period.

When Access is Granted

When access is granted, copies of the documents will be available for collection from Bagwan Health. Alternatively, the documents can be sent to the requester via post.

Application to Amend Personal Information

Should the person requesting access believe that the personal information held by Bagwan Health is incorrect, incomplete, and out of date or misleading, they can then apply to have that information amended.

Application to amend personal information must be in writing, and the applicant must provide as much information as they can to show how or why the records need to be amended. The onus to provide this evidence falls with the applicant, not Bagwan Health.

Written reasons will be provided to the requester if their amendment is refused, as well as information about how they can apply for a review of the decision.
