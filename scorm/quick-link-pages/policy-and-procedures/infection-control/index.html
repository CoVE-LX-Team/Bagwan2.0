Infection control

Policy Name – Infection control
 
 
Scope
This policy applies to all staff and contractors employed by Bagwan Health, Allied Health professionals, Credentialed Specialists, students, patients and visitors.
Policy 

Under the Occupational Health and Safety Act 2004, Bagwan Health is obligated to provide a safe workplace for their staff, including the provision of adequate infection control procedures and the right equipment and training.

Using personal protective equipment (PPE) and adhering to infection control procedures will reduce the transmission of pathogens such as bacteria, viruses, protozoa or fungi and provide protection for health care workers.

This policy defines the standards and processes associated with the use of personal protective equipment within the context of infection control only. Requirements for personal protective equipment outside of those used for infection control are not included in this procedure.

Procedure

 

Assumption of Risk

The basis of good infection control is to assume that everyone is potentially infectious and that proper procedures have to be followed at all times.

 

Personal Protection Equipment (PPE)

PPE is to be put on immediately prior to entering the room of an infectious patient and removed upon leaving the room. It is not to be worn throughout the ward, as this increases the risk of transmitting infection to patients.

PPE will be supplied and available in each clinical area or department at Bagwan Health. Staff shall wear the appropriate PPE whenever it is likely that they will come into contact with blood or body fluids from any patient. Personal protective equipment can include gowns, aprons, masks, gloves, protective eyewear and face shields.

Visitors are required to adhere to infection control policies by way of performing basic hand hygiene before and after visiting. If visiting patients in isolation, visitors are required to adhere to the specific instructions for the type of isolation, which includes gloves, aprons/gowns and masks when required. All visitors are encouraged to not visit other patients after they have visited an infectious patient.

Staff will be trained in the donning and removal of PPE in orientation.

 

Gowns

Gowns and aprons are worn to protect skin and/or clothing from becoming contaminated, soiled or wet from contact with blood or body substances.

Plastic aprons form part of the Routine Contact Precautions. Plastic gowns are long-sleeved and are required in instances where Intensive Contact Precautions are required.

The types of protective clothing used at Bagwan Health include:

·         Disposable plastic aprons which are suitable for most situations

·         Long-sleeved, disposable fluid-resistant gowns which are used when there is an elevated risk of contamination or for contact isolation.

Masks

Masks are worn when there is potential for the mucous membranes in the mouth and nose to be exposed to blood or body substances. Masks prevent the spread of micro-organisms from staff to patients and protect staff from inhaling infectious aerosols or droplets, smoke or plume or other airborne hazards.

The types of masks used at Bagwan Health include:

·         Surgical Masks which are used in wards, departments and operating theatres

·         N95 Particulate Masks which are used when caring for patients with suspected or diagnosed airborne infectious diseases.

 

Gloves

Gloves are worn when there is potential for the hands to be exposed to blood or body substances. When gloves are worn and used appropriately they reduce the likelihood of hand contamination and minimise the risk that a health care worker will become infected. Gloves also reduce the likelihood that staff will transmit micro-organisms from their hands to patients.

Single-use gloves in a range of sizes are available and accessible in all areas.

The types of gloves used at Bagwan Health:

·         Non-sterile gloves which are suitable for most situations

·         Sterile gloves which are worn for surgical procedures where asepsis must be maintained.

It is recommended that staff involved in surgical procedures double-glove for extra protection.

 

Protection Eyewear and Face Protection

Protective eyewear and face protection are worn when there is potential for the mucous membranes in the mouth, nose or eyes to be exposed to blood or body substances.

The types of eyewear and face protection used at Bagwan Health include:

·         Goggles

·         Safety glasses with side shields

·         Mask with attached visor

·         Chin length plastic face shield.

 

Safety Precautions

Appropriate safety precautions should be taken when donning and removing PPE. This includes hand hygiene and the proper handling of items in the patient environment.

 

Donning PPE

First, perform hand hygiene and then prior to entering the patient’s room, put on PPE in the following order:

1.    Gown

·         Fully cover torso from neck to knees, arms to wrists, and wrap around the back

·         Fasten in back at neck and waist.

2.    Mask

·         Ensure the coloured side is outward

·         Secure ties above and below ears or place the elastic loops around ears

·         Flatten flexible metal band over the nose

·         Fit snug to face and below the chin.

3.    Protective eyewear and face protection

·         Put over face and/or eyes and adjust to fit

4.    Gloves

·         Extend to cover wrist of gown.

 

Removing PPE

Remove PPE before leaving the patient’s room in the following order:

1.    Gloves

·         Grasp the outside of the glove with the opposite gloved hand and peel off

·         Hold the removed glove in the gloved hand

·         Slide fingers of un-gloved hand under remaining glove at the wrist

·         Discard into a yellow waste bag

·         Perform hand hygiene.

2.    Protective eyewear and face protection

·         To remove, handle by ‘clean’ headband or earpieces

·         Place in the designated receptacle for reprocessing

·         Perform hand hygiene.

3.    Gown

·         Unfasten neck, then waist ties

·         Using a peeling motion; pull gown from each shoulder toward the same hand

·         The gown will turn inside out

·         Hold gown away from body, roll into a bundle and discard into a yellow waste bag

·         Perform hand hygiene.

4. Mask

·         Grasp from bottom to the top of ties/elastics and remove

·         Place in waste receptacle

·         Perform hand hygiene

 

Handling Contaminated Sharps 

When handling needles and other sharp contaminated objects:

·         Handle by the barrel

·         Never attempt to re-cap or bend used needles

·         Place in an appropriate puncture-proof container – a yellow container, labelled ‘Danger contaminated sharps’ and marked with a black biohazard symbol.

 

Outbreak Management

Bagwan Health is required to implement an outbreak management plan when risk assessment identifies the presence of a serious disease that has the potential to spread within the hospital environment or community.

 

Identification of a Potential Outbreak

An outbreak may be identified by:

·         Staff

·         Monitoring systems

·         Laboratory data 

·         Regional Public Health, national or international alerts.

The Infection Prevention & Control Nurse and the Director of Clinical Services will be notified to instigate a risk assessment.

 

Risk Assessment

A risk assessment will be conducted to evaluate the certainty of the infection. This will be conducted by a Microbiologist or appropriate physician. 

The risks assessment will be based on the following guidelines:

·         Contagiousness of the disease

·         Severity of the disease

·         Number of people affected

·         Legal requirements for notification to the Medical Officer of Health

 

 

Key issues to be considered:

·         People with the infection are being appropriately cared for

·         Staff providing care are protecting their own health

·         Potentially exposed persons are correctly identified and informed

·         Risk of infection to others is reduced by implementing control measures.

 

Communicate and inform

The following staff must be informed:

·         Infection Prevention & Control Committee

·         Credentialed Specialists

·         Senior Nursing Team and Executive

·         Medical Officer of Health as required

·         Ministry of Health as required.

 

Formation of an Outbreak Committee

If an outbreak occurs, then an Outbreak Committee is formed to:

·         Gather information

·         Plan strategy

·         Implement control measures

·         Communicate with patients, staff, credentialed specialists, other appropriate external agencies. They may also assist the CEO with media management.

 

Evaluation

·         Debrief

·         Evaluation of the efficacy of outbreak prevention and control measures will be completed by the Infection Prevention and Control Nurse

·         Infection Prevention and Control nurse will provide a written report to the Infection Prevention and Control Committee.

 

Control Measures

Will be determined by the Outbreak Committee.

 


Appendix One: Types of Precautions for Infection Control 

Standard Precautions: For all patients at all times.

 

Component

Actions required

Hand hygiene

Hand hygiene is carried out effectively according to the ‘5 moments for hand hygiene’.

Personal Protective Equipment (PPE)

Proper use of PPE which includes:

  • Gloves worn by staff when there is contact with bodily fluids, blood, soiled equipment or linen or performing invasive procedures requiring asepsis
  • Plastic aprons or gowns are used to minimise the risk of staff exposure to bodily fluids
  • Masks, eye shields or safety goggles are worn when staff are at risk of a splash of bodily fluids or blood.

Management of sharps

Sharps are disposed of in the dedicated sharps bins located in each room. To minimise the risk of needlestick injuries staff must:

  • Use safety devices where appropriate and available
  • Never recap a used needle
  • Use a dedicated dish to transport the needle and avoid placing fingers into the dish with the used needle. The dish should be for the sharps alone, and not be used to carry other items
  • Dispose of the used needle immediately after use.        

Linen

Used linen should be handled as though it is contaminated with bodily fluids. This means that:

  • Appropriate PPE is worn when handling used linen
  • Linen skips are taken to the patient bedside and are used to dispose of the used linen before placing the linen skip back in its designated location
  • Each isolation room will have its own linen skip.

Patients

Patients are informed of the importance of hand hygiene.

Visitors

Visitors are to perform hand hygiene when entering and leaving the patient care areas.

 

 

 

Routine Contact Precautions: For patients isolated with VRE vanB, MRSA, ESBL and shingles limited to one dermatome.

Component

Action required

PPE
(staff and visitors)

Plastic apron and hand hygiene.

Patient location

Allocation of a single room is preferred with own bathroom. Patients with VRE vanB can be cohorted providing that they have not been on antibiotics for the past 6 months and are continent of faeces.

Patient equipment

Dedicated equipment for the length of stay is preferred. If the equipment is shared, cleaning is performed between patients as per standard precautions. Patient charts are to remain outside of the patient room.

Component

Action required

PPE
(staff and visitors)

Hand hygiene

Plastic apron

Gloves

Surgical mask if patient vomiting.

Patient location

Allocation of a single room with own bathroom.

Patient equipment

Dedicated equipment for the length of stay is preferred. If the equipment is shared, cleaning is performed between patients as per standard precautions. Patient charts are to remain outside of the patient room.

Action required

PPE

Patient equipment

Droplet precautions: For patients isolated with influenza, rubella or meningococcal disease.

Component

Action required

PPE
(staff and visitors)

Hand hygiene

Plastic apron

Surgical mask

Patient location

Allocation of a single room with own bathroom. Door to the room to remain closed.

 If a patient is located in a shared room, place a surgical mask on the patient, close the privacy curtains around the patient and ensure at least 1 metre between patients. Patient must be moved into a single room as soon as possible.

Patient equipment

Dedicated equipment for the length of stay is preferred. If equipment is shared, cleaning is performed between patients as per standard precautions. Patient charts are to remain outside of the patient room.


Airborne precautions: For patients isolated with pulmonary tuberculosis, chickenpox or measles.

Component

Action required

PPE
(staff and visitors)

Hand hygiene

N95 mask

Patient location

Allocation of a negative pressure isolation room with anteroom. Both sets of doors are to be kept closed to maintain the negative pressure.

Patient equipment

Dedicated equipment for the length of stay is preferred. If equipment is shared, cleaning is performed between patients as per standard precautions. Patient charts are to remain outside of the patient room.

Upon patient discharge

The room should remain in negative pressure for a minimum of 30 minutes of the patient leaving. If any staff are entering the room within this 30 minute period, they are to still wear the N95 mask.
