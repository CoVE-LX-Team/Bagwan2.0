Mandatory reporting

 
Policy Number
Version Number
Scope

This policy applies to all staff and contractors employed by Bagwan Health, Allied Health professionals, Credentialed Specialists, volunteers and students.

Policy

This policy has been developed, in conjunction with the Department of Health and Human Services (DHHS) Responding to Allegations of Physical or Sexual Assault Policy, the Victorian Child Safe Standards, the Victorian Reportable Conduct Scheme and the Australian Health Practitioner Regulation Agency (AHPRA) Guidelines for Mandatory Notifications.

The goal of this policy is to provide Bagwan Health staff, contractors, volunteers and students with information to assist them in managing and reporting allegations of physical or sexual assault/abuse and in making mandatory notifications under the Health Practitioner Regulation National Law.

Procedure

Allegations of Assault/Abuse

All allegations of physical or sexual assault/abuse at Bagwan Health must be reported to the Police, whether it is a staff member or a patient, and even if the victim has not consented to the matter being reported.

In addition, all allegations of assault/abuse e.g. emotional abuse, psychological abuse, physical abuse and sexual abuse, are considered a major incident, and as such need to be reported to DHHS within 24 hrs.

The following constitutes assault and/or abuse.

  • Physical assault/abuse

Which is when a person inflicts physical injury or unwanted physical contact upon a person by hitting, throwing, shaking, suffocating, strangulating, poisoning, mutilating, or assaulting with a weapon. Physical injury includes (but is not limited to) internal injuries, dislocated or broken bones, cuts, bruising, welts or burns.

  • Sexual assault/abuse 

Which is when a person uses power or authority over a person to involve the person in sexual activity. Physical force can be involved but not always. Sexual assault/abuse can involve a wide range of sexual activities including the fondling of a person’s genitals, masturbation, oral sex, vaginal or anal penetration by a penis, finger or other object and exposure of a child to pornography.

Other actions that may constitute assault/abuse includes spitting, threatening behaviour and an attempted assault.

There are also incidents that constitute assault under the law but do not cause physical injury. These may be best dealt with incident reporting rather than being reported directly to the Police e.g. one patient shoving another with no injury caused.

Indicators of a Possible Assault/Abuse

It may become apparent that an assault and/or abuse has occurred under various circumstances including:

  • A patient alleges that an assault has occurred
  • An employee, volunteer or student observes an incident
  • An employee, volunteer or student suspects that an incident has occurred,
  • e.g. a patient may be distressed and bruised, or clothes may be ripped
  • An employee, volunteer, student or visitor alleges assault by a patient
  • A patient’s behaviour changes significantly – this might include self-destructive behaviour, sleep disturbances, acting-out behaviours, being very quiet and/or withdrawn, or persistent and inappropriate sexual play
  • A patient complains of physical symptoms or symptoms appear – this might include abdominal pain, sexually-transmitted disease, or pregnancy.

Centres Against Sexual Assault (CASA)

Centres Against Sexual Assault (CASA) operate throughout Victoria and provide both crisis and ongoing counselling support to recent and past victims of sexual assault. All victims of sexual assault, whether female or male, have the right of access to CASA services.

Available CASA services include:

  • Immediate crisis counselling and support
  • Follow-up, longer-term counselling and support
  • Information regarding the victim’s options and rights within the legal system
  • Information regarding medical options, including follow-up medical treatment
  • Assistance in the management of sexually transmitted diseases and/or pregnancy arising from the assault
  • Assistance in the management of other practical consequences of the assault such as emergency housing and compensation
  • Support and information to friends and family members.

Responsibilities

If an employee, volunteer or student has a reasonable belief that an assault and/or abuse has occurred, they are responsible for informing the relevant manager as soon as possible.

The notified manager is responsible for:

  • Ensuring immediate medical assistance is arranged, if needed
  • Contacting the Police
  • Arranging for an Independent Third Person, if needed
  • Contacting the victim’s family/guardian, if the service user is under the age of 18
  • Ensuring the victim is offered counselling
  • Clearly documenting how the victim, if over the age of 18, demonstrated that they made an informed decision not to inform their family or guardian
  • Ensuring the victim does not have an enduring power of attorney and can make an informed decision, or their power of attorney is contacted.

In instances of alleged sexual assault and if the victim consents, the relevant manager is also responsible for contacting CASA. They will do this at the same time as they inform the Police of the allegation.

Reasonable Belief

A reasonable belief is formed if a person in the same position would have formed the same belief. It is based on factual information and observations. A reasonable belief is more than suspicion, there must be some objective basis for the belief, but it’s not the same as having proof and does not require certainty.

Legal Duty

Any adult who forms a reasonable belief that a sexual offence has been committed by an adult against a child under 16 has a legal duty to report that information to police. Failure to disclose the information to the police is a criminal offence. This offence is part of the Crimes Amendment (Protection of Children) Act 2014.

There are some exemptions and/or excuses to not report including where the information has already been disclosed.

There is also a mandatory requirement to report an assault to Police where the patient:

  • Is a statutory child protection service user
  • Resides in out-of-home care
  • Is a child/adult with a disability.

Child Abuse and Neglect Incidents

The Victorian Reportable Conduct Scheme requires the centralised reporting of allegations of child abuse (sexual, physical and psychological), from workers or volunteers in relevant organisations with a high level of responsibility for children. From 1 January 2018, the scheme was applied to hospitals, other disability services for children, providers of overnight camps, religious bodies and the residential facilities of boarding schools.

The reportable conduct scheme requires allegations of suspected criminal conduct to be reported to Victoria Police as the first priority and will maintain the privacy of an investigation by Victoria Police of any allegations of criminal misconduct.

To report concerns that are life-threatening, call Victoria Police on 000.

In reporting child sexual abuse to police, your identity will remain confidential unless:

  • You disclose it yourself or your consent in writing to your identity being disclosed
  • A court or tribunal decides that it is necessary in the interests of justice for your identity to be disclosed.

Bagwan Health CEO Responsibilities

The Bagwan Health CEO needs to be informed immediately of any child abuse or neglect incidents. The CEO (or a delegate) must report any allegation of reportable conduct to the Commission for Children and Young People (CCYP) within three business days of becoming aware of the allegation. It is a criminal offence for Bagwan Health CEO to fail to notify (within three business days) and update (within 30 calendar days) the CCYP about reportable allegations.

The reporting to the CCYP by the Bagwan Health CEO is in addition to all the current steps that are required to be undertaken when an allegation of child abuse (sexual, physical and psychological) or significant neglect is made.

What Should Happen

When an allegation is made, or an employee, volunteer or student becomes aware of an assault, immediate action should be taken to ensure the person’s safety and wellbeing. All action should be undertaken in a way that is respectful of the person, is culturally appropriate, and empowers them to make their own choices and decisions wherever possible.

The most senior staff member present should:

  • Assess the situation to ensure a safe environment
  • Contact the relevant manager and inform them of the situation, as soon as possible
  • Follow directions from the manager
  • Care for the victim, and give maximum support and assistance
  • Take reasonable precautions to preserve evidence and store records relating to a report of abuse/neglect securely and only permit access to authorised persons
  • Follow any directions given by Emergency Services – it may be necessary to preserve any forensic evidence.

Patients from Culturally and Linguistically Diverse Community

Where the patient uses a language other than English, an interpreter of the same sex as the patient should be arranged as soon as possible to interpret for the patient, Police and other persons involved in the process.

People from CALD communities may be reluctant to speak to an interpreter because they fear that what they say may be passed on to their local community. In this case, it is possible to request a telephone interpreter from another state or to not disclose the person’s name to the interpreter.

When using an interpreter directly, consideration should be given to arranging an interpreter who is not associated with them or their immediate cultural community.

ASA provides culturally-specific services available to victims from CALD communities.

Mandatory Notifications for Registered Health Practitioners

The National Law requires health practitioners, employers of health practitioners and education providers to report ‘notifiable conduct’ to the Australian Health Practitioner Regulation Agency (AHPRA) in order to prevent the public being placed at risk of harm.

Section 140 of the National Law defines ‘notifiable conduct’ as when a practitioner has:

  1. Practised the practitioner’s profession while intoxicated by alcohol or drugs; or
  2. Engaged in sexual misconduct in connection with the practice of the practitioner’s profession; or
  3. Placed the public at risk of substantial harm in the practitioner’s practice of the profession because the practitioner has an impairment; or
  4. Placed the public at risk of harm because the practitioner has practised the profession in a way that constitutes a significant departure from accepted professional standards.

Making a mandatory notification is a serious step and should only be taken on sufficient grounds.

Further information can be found in the AHPRA Guidelines for Mandatory Notifications.

Employee Assistance Program (EAP)

Bagwan Health is committed to maintaining a safe and healthy working environment and recognises the need to provide confidential, professional assistance for employees/volunteer’s experiencing work-related problems. This will be provided through access to an Employee Assistance Program (EAP).
