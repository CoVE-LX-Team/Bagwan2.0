Specimen collection and storage

Policy Name

Specimen Storage and Collection

Policy Number

010

Owner of Policy

Dr Christina Jones, CEO

Scope

All staff and contractors employed by Bagwan Health, Allied Health professionals, Credentialed Specialists and students who are responsible for collecting and/or storing specimens.

 

Policy 

The National Pathology Accreditation Advisory Council (NPAAC), the Royal College of Pathologists of Australasia (RCPA) and the Consumers Health Forum of Australia (CHF) identified the need for appropriate and documented specimen storage and collection processes as a high priority area where promoting best practice could lead to better patient outcomes.

This policy defines the standards and processes associated with the collection and storage of specimens at Bagwan Health.

 

Procedure

Pathology tests are an essential part of the health care system and are used to aid medical practitioners in the diagnosis of disease, assist in preventive health, acute care, management of chronic conditions and genetic research.

Pathology specimens provide essential information that assists patient management including determining appropriate treatment, which influences clinical outcomes of patients.

For the purposes of this policy, a specimen is any tissue or fluid from a patient that is submitted to Bagwan Health or external agency for analysis or testing. 

This can include, but is not limited to:

• Blood

• Urine

• Throat swabs

• Tissue biopsies

• Sputum

• Bodily fluids

• Faecal matter.

 

Request Forms for Testing

Bagwan Health provides request forms that must be completed for testing along with the supplies to collect the specimens. A request form must accompany each specimen. In order to provide accurate service, the request form and labels must be completed as described below.

 

Minimum Labelling Requirements

All patient information on the request form must match the patient information on the specimen labels. To comply with National Safety and Quality Health Service (NSQHS) standards, pathology request forms and specimens MUST have three identifiers:

• Patient’s full name – first name, surname and any middle names

• MRN or another unique patient identifier

• Date of Birth (DOB).

 

Clinical information must also be provided and will assist with determining the level of investigation required and the importance and prioritisation of unexpected results.

Labelling may include clinical details such as:

• Current problem

• Differential diagnosis

• Relevant medical history.

 

Contact details for the referrer must also be supplied for each request, this includes:

• Legible referrer name 

• Contact details e.g. phone number.

 

Note: If there is no label or the label is incomplete there will be no testing.

 

Specimen Relabelling

Specimen relabelling is not permitted. National Association of Testing Authorities (NATA) requirements reflect a tight policy on specimen relabelling. Since 1 October 2012, re-labelling of specimens has not been permitted.

Compliance will ensure maximum patient safety is a priority.

 

Specimen Relabelling

Pathology will not accept specimens that are unlabelled, mislabelled, mismatched or insufficiently labelled.

This is because unlabelled, mismatched or mislabelled specimens are the main sources of pre-analytical errors in the laboratory.

These types of errors can result in:

• Adverse patient outcomes including misdiagnosis and delayed, omitted or incorrect treatment

• Incorrect type of blood product being administered to the patient, which could lead to patient death

• Poor patient experience for patients who require a second sample to be recollected or the loss of the irreplaceable specimen

• Delays in forensic investigations and reports to families and coronial authorities.

 
Exceptions

For unidentifiable or unconscious patients, a minimum of two (2) patient identifiers must be used. This might include:

• MRN or other patient identifiers

• A further descriptor, for example, patient gender, motorbike accident.

Cases of unknown ID, or ID in situ, are managed by Victoria Police in the first instance. If a specimen is taken for the purposes of ID, the normal procedure for unknown or unidentified samples must be followed. In this case, other identifiers are used such as gender or a unique identifier.

 

Health and Safety Precautions

Specimens must be handled in a safe manner and according to applicable legal requirements. Use precautions when handling specimens containing blood or other potentially infectious material. Work areas contaminated with blood or serum must be disinfected immediately with 10% bleach (hypochlorite at 0.5% final concentration) or other approved disinfectant.

In the event of an exposure, administer first aid immediately, notify your manager or supervisor and seek prompt medical attention. First aid includes washing cuts and needle sticks with soap and water; flushing splashes to the nose, mouth, or skin with copious amounts of water; and irrigating eyes with clean water, saline, or sterile irrigants.

 

Collection a Specimen

Proper specimen collection and handling is an integral part of obtaining a valid and timely laboratory test result. Specimens must be obtained in the proper tubes or containers, correctly labelled, and then promptly transported to the laboratory.

The following steps are to collect a specimen for a patient:

1. Use the patient chart to confirm the patient’s identity and if applicable, scan the patient’s wristband using the barcode scanner.

2. Refer to the system to check what specimens were ordered.

3. Print the applicable specimen labels using the system.

4. Promptly collect labels from the printer.

5. Once the specimens are in their containers, initial and time and date the label/s.

6. Affix the appropriate label to the correct collection container. The barcode must be horizontal and straight, and over the existing label.

7. To communicate if the patient is fasting or if the specimen is urgent/critical, use the appropriate stickers and attach to the lid of the specimen. Write any additional comments relating to the collection on the specimen label or on a note placed in the specimen bag with the container.

8. Use the barcode scanner to scan the labels attached to the specimen containers. The system will confirm that the patient chart and the specimen container label are matched to the correct patient. The specimen order will be displayed with a tick to indicate it has been collected.

9. If a barcode scanner is not available it will be necessary to manually change the collected status of each of the specimens in the system. This can be done via the Status Menu in the system.

10. A warning message will display as the label was not scanned. Click ‘Yes’ once you have verified the container and label.

11. Marking specimens as Collected in the system will create an entry in AUSLAB for the tests indicated on the label.

12. Close system and send specimens to pathology.

 

Obtaining Reliable and Accurate Laboratory Test Results

Doctors and others responsible for obtaining specimens and transporting them to the laboratory have a vital role in ensuring that laboratory test results are valid. 

The following are essential safeguards for your patients:

• Avoid patient identification errors. Identify the patient prior to collecting a specimen using at least two patient identifiers. This could also be done by checking armbands. Acceptable identifiers may include the patient’s full name, date of birth, or medical record number.

• If applicable, draw the tubes in the proper sequence. When multiple tubes are to be drawn from a single venipuncture using an evacuated tube system, there is a correct sequence for blood collection that prevents cross-contamination of tube additives that could cause erroneous test results. The following should be used for both plastic and glass blood collection tubes. First collect blood in plain tubes, followed by gel tubes, then, liquid additive tubes.

• Use proper containers for collection. Using the wrong container often leads to erroneous results. Refer to the Swab Collection Guide and the Tube Collection Guide for exact requirements.

• Do not decant specimens from one type of container into another. Specimens must be submitted to the laboratory in the container used originally for collection.

• Promptly organise delivery of the specimens to the laboratory.

 

The Handling and Storage of Specimens 

Specimens should be stored and transported in a manner which maintains the viability of the specimen for the lifetime of the request. Generally, the specimen requirements are written in a format that specifies the requested volume, storage temperature, and any special handling notes. Blood tests that are required to be placed on ice, such as an arterial blood gas, must be taken immediately to the laboratory by a staff member, and not sent by the collection chute.

The requested volume is an amount sufficient to allow at least two performances of the assay either singly or in duplicate. The minimum volume allows one single analysis including instrument dead volume. 

Storage temperature is specified as room temperature (15 – 30°C), refrigerated (2 to 10°C) or frozen (-20°C or colder). When the temperature is not indicated, the sample may be stored and shipped in the most convenient manner.
